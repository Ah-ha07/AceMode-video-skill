# Opening and named-evidence motion

Use this reference when narration mentions a founder, executive, researcher, public figure, launch, keynote, official demo, paper, or product release, and whenever designing the first three seconds.

## Build an evidence target list

Before scene planning, extract editorially important named entities from the locked narration. For each entity, record:

- exact name and first spoken time;
- whether it is a person, event, product, paper, company, or place;
- preferred direct evidence;
- acceptable contextual fallback;
- source, license, attribution, and local asset path;
- planned scene and on-screen evidence label.

Use this search order:

1. Official press room, media kit, newsroom, launch page, keynote, demo, or owned channel.
2. Wikimedia Commons or another clearly licensed archive.
3. Licensed editorial photo or footage provider.
4. Verified document, screenshot, quote card, or source-page capture.
5. Generic contextual media only when direct evidence cannot be used and the role is labeled accurately.

Do not replace a known founder, speaker, event, or product with a generated lookalike. Image generation may supply abstract connective backgrounds, never identity evidence.

## Person treatment

At the first important mention of a person:

1. Show a ledgered contextual image or clip.
2. Introduce a clean alpha cutout in a separate depth plane.
3. Add a 4–10 px accent outline behind the cutout.
4. Animate one short identity cue: outline pulse, directional sweep, offset echo, or edge trace.
5. Settle into restrained parallax while a name-and-role lockup or proof card appears.

Use at most two outline colors from the project palette. Keep the main portrait natural; color treatment belongs on the duplicate alpha or backplate. Never cover the eyes, mouth, hands, or microphone with typography.

## Event and launch footage

When narration refers to a release, keynote, demo, interview, or announcement, search for the actual event. Prefer a short direct-evidence excerpt over generic technology B-roll. Select a meaningful internal start frame, normalize the clip, mute unused source audio, and label its evidence role.

Use footage in one of these patterns:

- full-bleed proof moment with a narrow source rail;
- 65/35 split between founder cutout and launch clip;
- moving video card behind a static person cutout;
- video-to-document match cut from announcement to technical report;
- event clip with one zoomed evidence inset, not several equal thumbnails.

If no usable clip can be licensed, use verified stills or documents and record the fallback. Never imply unrelated footage depicts the named event.

## First-three-second contract

Design the opening backward from frame 0:

- Frame 0 must be the publishable cover. It contains the dominant subject, a short headline, and one proof cue. Nothing essential waits for an entrance.
- Hold readability for the first 3–6 frames.
- Add one high-energy motion between frames 2–18: outline ignition, accent slash, quick parallax separation, or evidence-card snap.
- Reveal real evidence by 1.5 seconds.
- Land the first factual payoff by 3 seconds.
- Continue directly into the first explanatory beat; do not reset to a second title card.

Judge the cover at phone size and in grayscale. Judge the animation at frames 0, 8, 18, 45, and 90. Each checkpoint must preserve one clear focal point.
