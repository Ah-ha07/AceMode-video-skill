# Editorial documentary style grammar

## Visual identity

Use a warm paper field, near-black ink, one cool accent, one warm accent, white media borders, and restrained shadows. Suggested starting tokens:

| Role | Value |
| --- | --- |
| paper | `#F2EEE5` |
| ink | `#141414` |
| muted | `#6F726F` |
| red | `#E94443` |
| teal | `#2C9DA5` |
| yellow | `#E6B92B` |
| media border | `#FFFDF7` |

Use a heavy Chinese grotesk for headlines, a compact sans for metadata, and tabular numerals for scores and stats. Avoid glossy gradients, glass cards, neon glows, and UI-dashboard styling.

## Layout families

1. `full-bleed`: Match action or emotionally decisive footage. Keep headline and score in independent safe zones.
2. `stat-left`: Dominant media right, headline/stat rail left, optional evidence thumbnail low left.
3. `stat-right`: Dominant media left, headline/stat rail right, optional evidence thumbnail low right.
4. `portrait`: Large person crop with name/role outside the face area and one contextual inset.
5. `split`: Two related assets in unequal 65/35 proportions for comparison or cause/effect.
6. `poster`: Closing synthesis with one hero image, oversized word/number, and a small evidence card.
7. `cutout-portrait`: Transparent person cutout occupies 38–52% of the frame; oversized year and identity sit behind it; supporting proof card stays on the opposite side. Use for hooks, turning points, and person-led endings.
8. `document-network`: Recreated paper/document card plus a code-native diagram. Use for papers, inventions, algorithms, or causal explanation; never bake explanatory text into an AI-generated image.
9. `archive-video`: One dominant contextual or direct-evidence clip with a narrow editorial rail and explicit footage role label.

Do not use the same layout more than twice consecutively. Do not alternate layouts mechanically. Let the beat's communication job decide.

## Frame hierarchy

- Dominant media: 55–75% of frame area.
- Headline: 48–72 px at 1920×1080, 1–3 short lines.
- Kicker label: 14–20 px, solid accent block.
- Stat: 90–150 px, outside faces and action.
- Caption: 26–34 px, bottom-center black strip, one clause.
- Secondary media: 14–24% of frame, white border, ±0.5–2° rotation.
- Keep all critical content at least 70 px from frame edges; captions at least 34 px from the bottom.

## Motion grammar

- Dominant media: 18–30 frame spring entrance plus 2–6% scale drift during the hold.
- Headline: 12–22 frame vertical reveal after the media has established direction.
- Stat: 14–24 frame scale/slide; use a distinct direction from the headline.
- Secondary media: enter 8–18 frames after the stat.
- Person cutout: enter after the year field and before the supporting evidence card; use a 24–34 frame spring plus at most 1% idle drift.
- Person cutout identity cue: render a 4–10 px accent keyline from the alpha layer behind the subject, animate one brief outline pulse or directional sweep, then reduce it to a restrained static edge. Keep the face and hair readable; do not use a permanent neon glow.
- Video card: start on a meaningful internal frame, then hold or use a restrained 2–4% push; do not loop visibly inside a short beat.
- Caption: 10–16 frame vertical reveal; exit before the scene transition.
- Scene transition: 12–20 frame crossfade or directional mask. Use wipes only when movement or chronology motivates them.
- Never animate every element at once. A reliable order is media → headline → stat → evidence inset → caption.

## Cover-to-story opening

Treat frames 0–90 at 30 fps as one designed sequence:

1. Frame 0 is the cover. Show the final headline, dominant person or product, strong silhouette, and one proof image or meaningful video frame. Do not fade up from blank.
2. Frames 1–18 add controlled impact: a short accent-line sweep, outline pulse, or 2–4% parallax. The hierarchy remains readable.
3. Frames 12–45 reveal direct evidence: an official launch clip, event still, product demo, document, or sourced quote card.
4. Frames 36–90 convert the cover into the first explanatory layout while the first factual payoff lands.

Use one hero movement, one supporting evidence movement, and one typography movement. More simultaneous effects weaken the opening. Avoid logo montages, generic particle bursts, fake HUD graphics, and unrelated cinematic flashes.

## Rhythm

For a narrated 3-minute feature, use a repeating energy phrase such as:

`HOOK-fast / CONTEXT-hold / PROOF-fast / CHARACTER-slow / MATCH-fast-fast / TURN-hold / RESOLUTION-slow`

Within a beat, preserve at least one 1.2–2.5 second stable reading window. Motion should clarify hierarchy, not continuously decorate the frame.
