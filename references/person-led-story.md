# Person-led documentary route

Use this route when one person is the lens for a larger history. The success criterion is not a complete résumé; it is a causal arc in which personal choices expose why the field changed.

## Story spine

Build 7–9 beats:

1. Present-day consequence plus identity hook.
2. Formative question, environment, or early constraint.
3. The unfashionable choice or conflict.
4. The method, invention, or paper explained visually.
5. A setback, winter, rejection, or long hold.
6. The breakthrough and the collaborators who made it real.
7. Institutional scale or public consequence.
8. Resolution, reversal, warning, or unresolved meaning.

Do not turn the piece into isolated dates. Every year must change the causal state of the story.

## Evidence plan

Aim for this package before composition:

- one licensed high-resolution portrait suitable for an alpha cutout;
- one additional real portrait or event image for contrast;
- one direct-evidence document, paper, chart, or official record recreated as live typography when rights are unclear;
- one or two licensed machine/place/context video clips;
- code-native diagrams for mechanisms;
- generated historical environments only where licensed evidence is unavailable, always marked illustrative.

Use the cutout in at most three major beats. Repeating it in every scene weakens its editorial weight.

## Motion phrase

Use a deliberate energy sequence such as:

`CUTOUT hook / QUESTION hold / ARCHIVE reveal / DIAGRAM fast / WINTER slow / BREAKTHROUGH fast-fast / CONSEQUENCE split / PORTRAIT close`

Within a scene, reveal in causal order: year or place → dominant evidence → person → proof card → caption. Do not animate all layers simultaneously.

## Claims

Credit collaborators on-screen for papers, teams, and breakthroughs. Avoid “single genius invented everything” framing. Separate a verified biographical fact from an editorial inference in the research file.
